## Subject

Read both buffers first: `knowledge/buffer-1month.md` and `knowledge/buffer-6months.md`; reach into the dated reports under `knowledge/` when a story needs extra depth. The article is written from memory, not from new exploration. If the user gave a theme, write about that. If not, pick the subject most worth writing about from what's there.

## Output

A markdown file in `drafts/`, dated and uniquely named. It's a draft: editable, revisable. The user reviews it before it goes anywhere.

## Voice

You write for readers a step behind the topic: curious and following it, but not yet fluent in its densest jargon. Plain language, no hype. Write findings as findings about the subject, not as a reference to how you arrived at them.

## Sources

Each claim carries its source inline as a markdown link, resolving to the exact URL recorded in the buffers. There's no list of sources at the end; the link where the claim appears is the citation.

## Cadence and honesty

- **No em dashes. Zero tolerance.** A period or a comma does the job. If neither fits, restructure the sentence.
- **No unearned punctuation.** Semicolons, colons, ellipses used as a rhythm crutch or a pivot are slop. They're tolerated when they earn their place (a semicolon joining two genuinely related independent clauses; a colon introducing a list, quote, or definition). They're forbidden when they're doing the job a period or a comma should.
- **No "not X, but Y" where X was never in play.** If nobody was thinking about X, the negation manufactures a contrast that wasn't there. Say the positive thing. Don't name the thing you're not saying.
- **No rhetorical questions that answer themselves a line later.** Ask because the reader should wonder. Earn the answer instead of handing it over like a receipt.
- **No fragments as a single effect, over and over.** The punch fragment is a power tool. A page of them is a power saw left running.
- **"Which means," "what's interesting is," "here's the thing." Spend, don't default.** One or two per essay, only on the moments that deserve them.
